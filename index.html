Five layers in every photo.

Each one tells a stranger something about you. EXIF Pro shows them as layers you can read, edit or peel off.

L1

Location

The exact spot the photo was taken, down to a few metres. Often your home.

L2

Time

When it was taken. A few photos are enough to map a routine.

L3

Camera

Make, model and settings, enough to link photos to one device.

L4

Lens

The exact lens and focal length behind the shot.

L5

Author

Name and copyright, written in by cameras and editing apps.

Underneath the five layers, a Raw view lists every EXIF, IPTC, TIFF and GPS field in the file.

An app about privacy has to be private itself.

EXIF Pro reads and rewrites your photos entirely on your iPhone, using Apple's ImageIO and Vision. There is no account, no advertising and no third-party SDK, and photos never leave the device.

The app sends two things over the network: a version check to Apple and, unless you switch it off in Settings, anonymous usage counts tied to a random installation ID. Never your name, location, device identifiers or anything from a photo.

Good questions.

Does removing metadata change the picture itself?

Only the metadata changes. The clean copy keeps the full resolution and is saved at maximum quality, along with HDR and depth data.

What is the difference between Strip All and Secure Wipe?

Strip All removes the metadata blocks from the file. Secure Wipe first overwrites sensitive values (location, identity, dates) with zeros and then removes them, for cases where you want no trace of the original values.

Will it overwrite my original photos?

Not unless you ask. Save Copy writes a new photo to your library, and the app asks whether to keep or delete the original. You can set a default in Settings.

Does iPhone already remove location when I share?

Sometimes. Messages and some share targets strip GPS, but many apps, cloud drives, email attachments and marketplace uploads keep the full metadata. EXIF Pro lets you see and decide, instead of guessing.

Which formats are supported?

JPEG, HEIC and PNG photos from your library or imported files, plus other photo formats depending on their container. Live Photos are saved as a still copy. Video files are not supported.
